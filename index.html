CH 1

What is accounting?

Accounting turns business transactions into information people use to make decisions.

1. Interpret & recordWrite down each business transaction
→
2. ClassifyGroup similar transactions together
→
3. Summarize & communicateProduce reports for decision makers
External users (outside the company)

Owners, creditors, potential investors, unions, government agencies, suppliers, customers, the public.

They read the 4 financial statements: Balance sheet, Income statement, Statement of retained earnings, Statement of cash flows.

Internal users (inside the company)

Board of directors, CEO, CFO, vice presidents, business-unit, plant and store managers, supervisors.

The CFO oversees the Controller (accounting) and the Treasurer (cash & financing).

Goal of external reporting: help people make investment and credit decisions, judge the amount, timing and uncertainty of future cash flows, and see the company's resources and claims on them.

Memory trick "DEA-LER": Dividends, Expenses, Assets go up with a debit. Liabilities, Equity, Revenues go up with a credit. Debit = left side, credit = right side.

The 5 steps to record a transaction

1Identify the accounts and their type (A, L, E)
2Increase or decrease? Apply the debit/credit rules
3Write the entry in the journal (date order)
4Post it to the ledger (one page per account)
5Check that A = L + E still balances

Debit always written first; the credit line is indented.

Key principles

Time period principle: measure income over short, equal periods.

Matching / revenue recognition: record revenue when goods are sold or services done; record expenses in the period they helped earn revenue.

Accrual basis: what matters is when it is earned or used, not when cash moves.

Retained earnings = all profits since the start − all dividends paid.

Trial balance check: lists every account balance (assets first, then liabilities, then equity). If debits ≠ credits: look for a missing account, divide the difference by 2 (an amount put on the wrong side), divide it by 9 (digits swapped, e.g. 85 written 58). A balanced trial balance can still hide errors, such as using the wrong account.
CH 4

Adjusting entries

At the end of each period, fix the accounts so revenues and expenses land in the right period. This was Exercise 2 of your midterm (20 pts). The Chapter 4 slides weren't uploaded, so this summary follows your midterm.

Two rules: an adjusting entry never touches Cash, and it always pairs one income statement account with one balance sheet account.
1 · Prepaid expense: asset → expense

Paid 3,600 on Oct 1 for one year of insurance, debited to Prepaid insurance. One month = 3,600 ÷ 12 = 300.

Dr Insurance expense 300 · Cr Prepaid insurance 300

2 · Unearned revenue: liability → revenue

Received 1,200 on Dec 1 for 3 months of services, credited to Unearned revenue. December earned = 1,200 ÷ 3 = 400.

Dr Unearned revenue 400 · Cr Service revenue 400

3 · Accrued expense: used, not yet paid

Salaries 1,500/week (5 days) = 300/day. Last payday Friday Dec 26; Dec 29, 30, 31 unpaid = 3 days → 900.

Dr Salaries expense 900 · Cr Salaries payable 900

4 · Accrued revenue: earned, not yet billed

Services done in December, not billed or recorded: 800.

Dr Accounts receivable 800 · Cr Service revenue 800

5 · Depreciation: asset → expense (an example of type 1)

Machine 18,000, 4-year life, adjusted every month: 18,000 ÷ (4 × 12) = 375 per month.

Dr Depreciation expense 375 · Cr Accumulated depreciation 375

Read the frequency. "The company records adjusting entries at the end of each month": the December entry covers one month only, because earlier months were already adjusted. "Only once a year": count all the months since the start (insurance bought Oct 1 → 3 months = 900).

Credit terms "2/10, n/30" (expert part)

Pay within 10 days and get a 2% discount; otherwise the full amount is due in 30 days. Example: $10,000 purchase.

CH 7

Financial assets

Cash and anything that turns easily into a known amount of cash: short-term investments and receivables.

Cash

Coins, notes, cheques, card sales, money orders: anything the bank accepts as a deposit.

Cash equivalents

Very safe, stable value, matures within 90 days. E.g. money-market funds, Treasury bills.

Restricted cash / credit line

Restricted cash can't be used for daily needs. A line of credit only becomes a liability once you draw on it.

Internal control over cash: the person who handles cash never keeps the records · deposit receipts daily · avoid paying in cash (except petty cash) · separate approving from paying · prepare cash budgets · reconcile the bank statement promptly.

2. Estimate %Older = higher % uncollectible

Adjusting entry (Ranch Valley)

Needed allowance 5,680 − existing 4,000 = 1,680

Allowance = contra-asset. Net realizable value = A/R − Allowance (250,000 − 10,000 = 240,000).

Write-off & recovery

Write-off (4,000): Dr Allowance / Cr A/R. Net realizable value stays 240,000 and no expense is booked.

Recovery (500), two entries: ① Dr A/R / Cr Allowance (reverse the write-off), ② Dr Cash / Cr A/R.

Expert part: marketable securities (Foster buys Coca-Cola shares)

Cost includes commission: (4,000 × 48.98) + 80 = 196,000, so $49/share.

Sell above cost → Gain 500 sh: proceeds 25,000 − cost 24,500 = gain 500.

Sell below cost → Loss 2,500 sh: proceeds 120,000 − cost 122,500 = loss 2,500.

Year end: value the remaining shares at market: 49,000 → 47,000 = unrealized holding loss 2,000.

When prices are rising

FIFO: lowest COGS, so the highest profit (and higher taxes). Inventory is valued at recent costs.

LIFO: highest COGS, so the lowest profit, the most conservative method. Inventory is valued at old costs. IFRS prohibits LIFO.

Specific ID: logical when units are unique (cars, art).

Other rules

Consistency: keep the same method every year. You can change it only if you explain why and disclose the effects.

Physical count: adjusts the records for shrinkage (theft, breakage, spoilage). Obsolete goods are written down to zero or scrap value. In both cases: Dr COGS / Cr Inventory.

On the balance sheet, inventory comes right after accounts receivable.

CH 9

Plant & intangible assets

Long-lived assets are spread as an expense over the years they help earn revenue (matching principle).

Tangible

Depreciation

Buildings, equipment, furniture, land improvements. Land is never depreciated.

Intangible

Amortization

Patents, copyrights, goodwill. No physical substance, often exclusive rights.

Natural resources

Depletion

Oil, timber, minerals. Dr Depletion expense / Cr Natural resources.

What goes into the cost?

Purchase price + everything reasonable and necessary to get the asset to its location and ready to use: transport, insurance in transit, taxes, installation, testing, legal fees, clearing land.

Lump-sum purchase: split the total by relative market values.

Spending after purchase

Capital expenditure increases capacity, efficiency or useful life → added to the asset.

Revenue expenditure ordinary maintenance → expense now.

Two depreciation methods: S&G truck (cost 17,000, residual 2,000, 5 years)

Straight-line

(17,000 − 2,000) ÷ 5 = 3,000 / year

Same expense every year.

Double-declining balance

Book value × (1/5 × 2) = BV × 40%

Year 1: 17,000 × 40% = 6,800. Ignore residual value until the last year, where expense = BV − residual (2,203 − 2,000 = 203).

Straight-line book valueDouble-declining book value
Journal entry & where it shows

Dr Depreciation expense 3,000 → income statement

Cr Accumulated depreciation 3,000 → balance sheet, contra-asset

Book value = Cost − Accumulated depreciation

Special cases

Partial year: bought July 1 → 6/12 of a year. Half-month rule: bought on/before the 15th counts the full month.

Revised estimate: (BV now − residual) ÷ remaining life. Truck after 3 years: (8,000 − 2,000) ÷ 5 = 1,200/yr.

Consistency: don't switch methods from year to year.

Disposal (expert part): compare cash received with book value

Cash > BV → Gain

Sell truck (BV 2,000) for 3,000: gain 1,000 (credit).

Cash < BV → Loss

Sell for 1,000: loss 1,000 (debit). Discard for 0: loss 2,000.

Cash = BV

No gain or loss.

Steps: ① update depreciation to date ② remove the asset (Cr cost) and its accumulated depreciation (Dr) ③ record cash ④ plug the gain or loss.
